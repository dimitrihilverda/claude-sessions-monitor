Flash the Cheap Yellow Display

Puts Claude Sessions Monitor firmware on an ESP32-2432S028R, straight from this page. No Arduino IDE, no toolchain. The display then shows your live Claude Code sessions and which one is waiting for you — over Wi-Fi, or over the USB cable when the network will not cooperate.

Plug the display into USB, then press the button and pick its serial port.

This browser cannot flash. Flashing needs Web Serial, which today means desktop Chrome or Edge. Firefox, Safari and mobile browsers do not support it. The page is safe to read in any browser — only the button needs Chrome or Edge.

Before you press it

  • A USB data cable. Plenty of cables only carry power. If the port never appears, that is the first thing to swap.
  • The CH340 driver. Most systems have it; Windows sometimes does not. If no serial port shows up, install it from WCH.
  • Close the Arduino IDE and any serial monitor. Only one program can hold the port — and if you already run the PC service, release the port from the HUD menu first (see below).

What happens after flashing

You do not enter your Wi-Fi password here, and it is not baked into the firmware. On first boot the display brings up its own network:

Join it with your phone — on most phones the page opens by itself. Pick your network, type the password, and fill in the address of the PC running the web service. Settings are stored in NVS, so they survive reflashing: after the first time you never have to do this again.

To change them later, hold your finger on the top bar of the display for two seconds. That is the way back in when the display is on Wi-Fi but the PC address has gone stale — in that case the portal never appears on its own.

What you get

Tap a rowRaises that session's terminal window on your PC
Tap the top barSteps the brightness, six levels
Hold the top barTwo seconds opens the setup page again
Three buttonsApprove, reject, snooze — and you decide what they mean, on the PC, without reflashing
ArrowsAppear past four sessions, to scroll the list

Up to sixteen sessions are kept, four shown at a time. A session that starts asking for you scrolls itself into view, so a quiet screen really does mean nothing wants you.

All the text follows your Windows display language, and it comes from the PC rather than the firmware — so changing language does not mean flashing again. Dutch and English ship today.

If your network blocks the port

Office networks tend to block a port like 8787, and guest networks often keep devices from talking to each other at all. There is then nothing for the display to poll — while it hangs off the laptop by a cable that could carry the same data.

So it does. The service on your PC pushes the identical payload over this same USB cable, and the display prefers it whenever it is arriving. Nothing to configure: plug it in and it uses the cable, unplug it and it goes back to Wi-Fi by itself. The header shows USB next to the clock so you can tell which one you are on.

One consequence: while the service is feeding the display it holds the COM port, so flashing from this page will fail with a port error. The HUD's right-click menu has “Release the USB port” for exactly that — it lets go for a minute and reattaches on its own.

It flashed, and the screen is white

Then the panel is fine but its settings are not. This build ships the right User_Setup.h, so a white screen after flashing from this page points at a different board revision. The troubleshooting table walks through white screens, inverted colours and mirrored displays.

You still need the PC side

The display has nothing to show on its own: it asks a small web service on your PC every three seconds. That service, the floating HUD and the Claude Code hooks live in the repository — start with the quick start.
